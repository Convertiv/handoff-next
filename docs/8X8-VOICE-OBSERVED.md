# 8x8 brand voice, observed from the live site

**Gathered 2026-07-30** from `/products/unified-communications`, `/solutions/connect-your-people`, and
`/resources/customer-stories/gillette-childrens`.

The voice currently in Design.MD was invented for the demo. This is what 8x8 actually writes. Where the
two disagree, the site wins — a voice check that flags real 8x8 copy is worse than no voice check,
because it teaches people to ignore it.

## Where the fabricated guidance is wrong

| Invented rule | What the site does |
|---|---|
| *"Seamless"* is banned as overused | Used freely: **"Integrate seamlessly."** is a section heading, **"deliver seamless continuity"** is body copy, and the UC page's own `<title>` is *"8x8 Unified Communications \| Seamless Collaboration"* |
| *"Empower"* only for agents | **"the platform made to empower modern CX leaders"** |
| *"Transform your business"* banned | **"Transforming care with communication"** is a section heading |
| CTAs must be *"imperative verb first"* | Half are noun phrases: **"Platform Overview"**, **"All Features"**, **"Quick Links"** |
| Headlines *"3–8 words, never more than 10"* | Roughly right — observed range is 2–9 |

Genuinely absent from the site, so probably safe to keep discouraging: *synergy, revolutionize, disrupt,
game-changing, best-in-class.* Nothing observed contradicts those.

## What is actually distinctive

Four patterns the invented guidance missed entirely, and they do more to make copy sound like 8x8 than
any word list.

**1. Problem first, then resolution.** Name the pain in a short sentence, then fix it.

> "When communication breaks down, business slows down. Fragmented tools and data silos make
> collaboration harder, decisions slower, and customer experiences inconsistent."

> "Disconnected tools slow everything down—decisions, conversations, and momentum. We bring voice,
> video, chat, and messaging together on one secure platform, so teams stay aligned, workflows run
> smoother, and customers never get left hanging."

**2. Triads.** Three-part lists, constantly, usually to close a sentence.

> "decisions, conversations, and momentum" · "teams stay aligned, workflows run smoother, and customers
> never get left hanging" · "collaboration harder, decisions slower, and customer experiences
> inconsistent"

**3. Conversational idiom in headings.** This is the thing that most separates real copy from
generated copy.

> "The numbers that do the talking." · "Six reasons people stop shopping around." · "See it before you
> decide." · "Good questions. Straight answers." · "customers never get left hanging"

**4. Fragment headings with a full stop.** Sentence case, terminal period, often not a full sentence.

> "Enterprise, simplified" · "Secure and compliant." · "Cloud-first, future-focused"

## Observed inconsistency worth deciding about

Heading case is **not consistent between templates**. The UC product page uses sentence case with
periods (*"What your teams unlock with 8x8 Work."*); the solutions page uses Title Case
(*"Connect Your People Without the Complexity"*, *"Why Connection Matters"*). Both are live. Either
pick one for generated pages or leave it to the page type — but do not assert a rule the site does not
follow.

## Vocabulary they actually reach for

CX · CX leaders · CX ambitions · one platform · one secure platform · cloud-first · silos · data silos ·
clarity · confidence · momentum · aligned · Global Reach™ · intelligent call routing · 99.999% uptime ·
Patented · unified communications

## Caveat

Three pages is a small sample, weighted toward product and solution templates. Pricing, partner and
support pages may read differently. Worth widening before treating this as canonical — but it is
already far closer to the truth than what is in Design.MD today.
